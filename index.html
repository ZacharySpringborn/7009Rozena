01 The Opportunity

One of the last private lakes on the Front Range.

Spanning roughly fifty contiguous acres of Boulder County, the Rozena assemblage is anchored by a 16.6-acre, spring-fed private lake and the rare ownership of 1.36 shares of the Palmerton Ditch. It pairs an established, move-in residence with open, buildable ground — the unusual chance to live on the land while you build your own home on it.

The offering is four distinct parcels marketed as a single legacy estate: an existing 1986 contemporary residence with an equestrian barn, pool and orchards; an adjacent open hay parcel; the lake parcel itself with a designated shoreline building envelope; and a conservation outlot that permanently shields the compound's privacy. Pastoral seclusion within minutes of Longmont, framed by Longs Peak.

* "Potential home sites" reflects the existing residence plus two buildable envelopes described in the listing. Both new-build envelopes are listing representations and must be verified with Boulder County — see Due Diligence.

Why this fits the brief

Live on the land while you build the home you want.

Ray's goal has been a multi-structure compound where he can occupy an existing residence while building new. Rozena is rare in offering exactly that on a single assemblage: a move-in home today, plus open ground the listing presents as two additional build envelopes — all wrapped around a private lake. It mirrors the build-while-residing path we mapped at Eagle Ridge, but here the existing dwelling already stands and the water is the centerpiece.

The decisive question is entitlement: whether Boulder County's Agricultural zoning, Residential Floor Area (RFA) caps, and Limited Impact / Site Plan Review will actually permit additional dwelling(s) at the sizes marketed. That is the first thing to pressure-test — see Due Diligence.

07 The Balanced View

Highlights & Concerns.

An honest ledger — the things that make this singular, and the items that warrant scrutiny before you fall in love with it.

+ Highlights The Upside

  • A private lake. A ~16.6-acre spring-fed lake with a trophy fishery is nearly impossible to replicate on the Front Range.
  • Real water. 1.36 shares of the Palmerton Ditch plus a ditch-fed pond — productive, conveyable water (subject to verification).
  • Move-in plus build-new. An existing residence to occupy day one alongside open ground for new construction — directly on the brief.
  • Established infrastructure. Equestrian barn with office & stalls, pool & pool house, greenhouses, orchards and gated private-road access already in place.
  • Permanent privacy. The 9.69-ac conservation outlot guarantees the seclusion can never be built out around you.
  • Setting & views. Unobstructed Longs Peak and water views; lake parcel mapped Flood Zone X (minimal hazard).
  • Unincorporated & agricultural. No municipal overlay; A-zoning supports equestrian and agricultural use, with low ag-classified taxes today.

– Concerns To Pressure-Test

  • Two ownerships, four parcels. The lake parcel is held by the Hepp Gayle G Trust; the other three by GH Land LLC. Confirm all four convey together and how title is structured.
  • Buildability is unverified. The ≈5,500 & ≈6,500 sq ft envelopes are marketing claims. A-zoning RFA caps and LISR/SPR could limit additional dwellings — verify with the County first.
  • Conservation easement. Outlot B (and a broader 43.4-ac easement per the original NUPUD) permanently restricts development — confirm the exact encumbered area and terms.
  • Severed minerals. The lake parcel notes partial mineral severance (oil/gas & sand/gravel). Identify who holds rights and whether any leases create surface-use risk.
  • Dated improvements. The residence dates to 1986 with largely original interiors — budget for modernization.
  • Record discrepancies. MLS sq ft (8,124) and bed count (4) exceed assessor records (~6,043 finished; 3 BR); the listing address (7009) is the vacant lot.
  • Utilities & lake duties. Septic/well dependence (new builds need OWTS & well permits); a 16.6-ac impoundment may carry dam-safety and maintenance obligations.
  • Value gap. $15.75M list vs ~$4.34M combined assessor actual value — the premium is for land, water & potential, not the structures; it needs comp support.

10 Due Diligence

What to confirm before an offer.

The path to a clean offer runs through these answers. I'll lead the verification — several can begin with a pre-application consult with Boulder County Community Planning & Permitting (303-441-3930).

1

Conveyance of all four parcels

Written confirmation the Hepp Trust lake parcel sells together with the three GH Land LLC parcels; review title commitment and transaction structure.

2

Buildability & entitlements

County pre-app: how many dwellings, and at what size, A-zoning permits across the parcels — RFA caps, LISR / Site Plan Review triggers, setbacks from water.

3

Conservation easement scope

Obtain the recorded easement (NUPUD SD-88-0024 / Outlots A & B) and confirm exactly what is restricted and whether it touches any build envelope.

4

Water rights & wells

Confirm 1.36 Palmerton Ditch shares (and any wells) convey; ditch-company transfer process; augmentation needs for new wells.

5

Lake & dam obligations

Determine State Engineer / dam-safety jurisdiction for the impoundment, plus ongoing maintenance, water-quality and liability duties.

6

Mineral & surface rights

Title search on the severed oil/gas & sand/gravel interests; check for active leases and any surface-use exposure.

7

Septic & soils for new builds

OWTS feasibility and well permits for additional residences; review the existing soil report (BPR-18-0018) and order new percolation work as needed.

8

Survey, floodplain & structures

ALTA survey / ILC; confirm build envelopes relative to floodplain near the lake; inspect the 1986 residence, barn and pool; scope renovation.
